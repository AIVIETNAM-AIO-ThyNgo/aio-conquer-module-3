## Verdict up front

This is the strongest-evidenced component in the pipeline so far. Unlike the LightGBM stage (previously audited: **Fail**, duplicate implementation, dropped seed), the splitter has exactly **one implementation**, imported unchanged by five separate consumers (`run_baseline.py`, `train_lightgbm.py`, `validate_walk_forward.py`, `audit_oos_split_integrity.py`, and `E3-S2/pipeline/model.py`), never modified since its single creation commit, and independently re-derived and re-checked by two separate downstream scripts using label-outcome-date arithmetic rather than trusting the splitter's own internal variables. §4 (purge correctness) and §10 (meta-leakage on the splitter's own design) — the two sections this audit was told to distrust hardest — hold up under direct arithmetic verification, not just documentation.

**One honest limitation on my own verification**: this session's shell has no `pandas`/`numpy` installed, so I could not execute `purged_walk_forward_splits()` live and print fresh fold arrays myself. What I did instead — re-deriving the exact index arithmetic from the source and checking it, row-for-row, against two independently-generated, already-committed artefacts (`E2-S3`'s `fold_boundary_audit.csv` and `E4-S2`'s `integrity_gate_report.json`, produced by two different scripts, on two different dates, both re-deriving purge sufficiency from the label's own price reference rather than calling into `splits.py`'s internals) — is strong corroborating evidence, but it is not the same as a from-scratch execution in this session. Flagged here rather than silently upgraded to "I ran it myself."

## 1. Strict temporal ordering — **Pass**

`purged_walk_forward_splits` builds `train_idx = np.arange(0, purge_start_idx)` and `test_idx = np.arange(test_start_idx, test_end_idx)` with `purge_start_idx = test_start_idx - horizon`. Since `horizon = 5 > 0`, `purge_start_idx < test_start_idx` always holds by construction, so `max(train_idx) < min(test_idx)` strictly, for every fold, as an algebraic identity — not dependent on the data. Cross-checked against the real, committed `fold_boundary_audit.csv` for all 6 folds: `train_end_date` is strictly before `test_start_date` in every row (e.g. fold 0: train ends 2011-01-25, test starts 2011-02-02; fold 5: train ends 2023-12-22, test starts 2024-01-03) — checked first, a middle, and the last fold explicitly, per the standard's instruction, not just fold 0. `E2-S1/tests/test_baseline_zero.py::test_folds_cover_the_oos_region_with_no_gap_or_overlap` and `E2-S3/tests/test_walk_forward_validation.py::test_chronological_order_holds_for_every_real_fold` both assert this over the *entire* fold list, and the latter has an adversarial counterpart (`test_chronological_order_catches_an_inverted_fold`) proving the check isn't vacuous. Date type: confirmed timezone-naive throughout — `E3-S2/pipeline/data_foundation.py:128` and the source notebook both call `.tz_localize(None)` immediately after loading raw OHLCV, and dates are daily (no time component) end to end, so no timezone or granularity artefact can slip a same-day boundary through as `<`.

## 2. No shuffling — **Pass**

The splitter itself takes no seed and contains no randomness — it is a pure function of `(dates, n_folds, min_train_size, horizon)`, verified by reading the full function body: no `np.random`, no `sklearn` CV object, no `shuffle` parameter anywhere in it. It actively rejects unsorted input (`if not dates.is_monotonic_increasing: raise ValueError`), tested directly by `test_splits_reject_unsorted_dates`. Repo-wide grep for `shuffle=True`, `KFold`, `train_test_split`, `TimeSeriesSplit` across every `.py`/`.ipynb` in the project (excluding the unrelated vendored `rcs/` directory) returned **zero hits** — nothing downstream reorders rows before they reach the model. LightGBM's own `subsample`/`colsample_bytree` (row/column subsampling *within* a fold during boosting) is correctly a separate, expected form of stochasticity and is not conflated with fold assignment anywhere in this audit.

## 3. Fold design is documented, not just implied by code — **Pass**

`E2-S1_Baseline_Zero_Predictor/README.md` states the design explicitly and unambiguously: **expanding** train window (confirmed in code — `train_idx` always starts at `0`; confirmed in `test_train_size_expands_across_folds`), `N_FOLDS = 6`, `MIN_TRAIN_SIZE = 1260` ("~5 trading years," with the same rationale echoed in `pipeline_config.yaml`'s comment), contiguous non-overlapping test blocks, and the purge length stated as tied directly to `HORIZON_TRADING_DAYS = 5` — "a train row within `HORIZON_TRADING_DAYS` (5) of a test block's first date has a label window that overlaps the test period," citing the specific prior audit finding (`E4-S1`) that motivated it, not an arbitrary round number.

## 4. Purge correctness — **Pass** (the section this audit was told to distrust hardest)

**The literal rule**: `purge_start_idx = test_start_idx - horizon`; train uses `[0, purge_start_idx)`. Re-derived independently: the last training row actually used is at index `test_start_idx - horizon - 1`; its label (`Close[i+5]/Close[i]-1`, confirmed by `E4-S1`'s `test_target_matches_manual_forward_return`) reads a price at index `(test_start_idx - horizon - 1) + horizon = test_start_idx - 1` — one index *before* the test window starts, never inside it. This is purging on the **label's outcome date**, not merely the feature date — exactly the distinction the standard warns is easy to get subtly wrong.

Critically, this was not taken on faith from `splits.py`'s own variable names. Two independent downstream scripts re-derive purge sufficiency from first principles and would fail if `splits.py`'s arithmetic were ever wrong:
- `E2-S3/validate_walk_forward.py::assert_purge_removes_label_overlap` computes `label_price_idx = last_train_idx + horizon` and asserts `label_price_idx < test_start_idx`, explicitly documented as "independent of the `purge_start_idx` arithmetic inside `splits.py`." Backed by an adversarial pair of tests: `test_purge_check_catches_an_under_purged_boundary` (gap of 3 rows, correctly raises) and `test_purge_check_accepts_an_exactly_sufficient_boundary` (gap of exactly 5, correctly passes) — the exact boundary case the standard demands, not a general assertion taken on faith.
- `E4-S2/audit_oos_split_integrity.py` independently computed, per fold, `label_price_idx = last_train_idx + horizon` and confirmed `< first_test_idx` for **all 6 folds** (`integrity_gate_report.json`, `purge_gap.passed = true`, every fold `effective: true`), on a separate run, on a separate date.

Reapplication at every boundary, not just the first: confirmed algebraically — `purge_start_idx` is recomputed from that fold's own `test_start_idx` inside the loop for every `k`, so as the expanding window absorbs a prior fold's test block into the next fold's train set, the purge is freshly re-cut at the new boundary, not inherited stale from fold 0. Verified against real numbers: fold 1's `train_end_date` (2013-08-26) sits exactly 5 trading rows before fold 1's own `test_start_date` (2013-09-04), not fold 0's.

**Trading-day vs calendar-day purge**: the horizon is trading-day-based (rows are already trading days only), so index-based purging is only equivalent to a genuine 5-*trading*-day purge if there are no silently missing trading days between consecutive rows. This is checked explicitly, not assumed: `assert_dates_are_contiguous_trading_days` flags any row-to-row gap exceeding 7 calendar days, with a documented empirical basis (the longest legitimate holiday gap observed in this dataset is 5 calendar days) — and the code is honest about its own residual blind spot: it "cannot, by construction, detect a single missing trading day hidden inside an already-long holiday gap." That residual is real and disclosed, not hidden, and does not require an authoritative trading calendar this repo doesn't have; noted here as a genuine but narrow unverified edge, not a failure.

**Feature-side leakage (secondary check)**: `E4-S1`'s audit (independently, before `E2-S1` existed) rebuilt all 11 features from raw OHLCV truncated at `t` and matched the published values exactly at 14 sampled dates; `E4-S2`'s gate separately re-confirmed "all features are right-aligned (trailing windows only)" per fold, for all 6 folds. No forward-looking feature construction found.

## 5. Preprocessing/model fitting occurs only on the training fold — **Pass**

No stateful preprocessing (scaler, imputer, feature selector, PCA, target encoder) exists in the live training path for either model implementation — `train_lightgbm.py` and `E3-S2/pipeline/model.py::run_lightgbm` both read raw feature columns straight from the canonical CSV with no transform step, appropriate for tree-based models. The one `StandardScaler` in the entire repository, `E2-S3/validate_walk_forward.py::fit_scaler_on_train_fold_only`, is explicitly a forward-looking guard for a *future* model that might need scaling — its own docstring states "LightGBM (E2-S2) needs none, so this guard has no caller yet in this repo" — and it is fit correctly, `scaler.fit(df.loc[fold.train_idx, columns])`, train-only. It is exercised only in tests, not in any production path, so there is currently no live preprocessing-leakage surface to find. Feature-selection/fold-drift tension (the second half of this section) does not apply: the feature list is fixed by `E1-S4`/`E1-S6` upstream of the splitter and is never refit per fold anywhere in this codebase.

## 6. Edge case — overlapping 5D labels at the boundary — **Pass**

Exercised directly, not merely claimed: `test_purge_check_catches_an_under_purged_boundary` constructs a synthetic fold with only a 3-row gap (< horizon) and confirms it is rejected; `test_purge_check_accepts_an_exactly_sufficient_boundary` constructs a fold with a gap of *exactly* 5 rows and confirms it is accepted. Both are boundary cases in the literal sense the standard asks for, not general-rule assertions.

## 7. Edge case — too-small early training window — **Pass**

Documented minimum: `MIN_ADEQUATE_FIRST_FOLD_TRAIN_SIZE = 1000` in `validate_walk_forward.py`, with a stated rationale (fold 0's window is `MIN_TRAIN_SIZE - HORIZON_TRADING_DAYS` after the purge shrinks it, and 1000 is the floor below which that shrink would be inadequate rather than expected). The actual first fold has `1255` training rows — comfortably above the floor — verified directly in the committed `walk_forward_validation_summary.json` (`first_fold_train_size: 1255`, `first_fold_train_size_adequate: true`) and re-derived algebraically (`1260 - 5 = 1255`). Policy on violation is not silent: `compute_verdict()` sets the overall verdict to `BLOCKED` if this check ever fails, tested directly by `test_compute_verdict_blocks_on_an_inadequate_first_fold`.

## 8. Edge case — final partial fold — **Pass**

Policy is explicit and consistently applied, not an off-by-one accident: `test_end_idx = n if k == n_folds - 1 else test_start_idx + test_size` — the **last** fold absorbs the integer-division remainder rather than being dropped or left short. In this dataset that makes the last fold slightly *larger* (655 rows) than the other five (650 each), confirmed in both `fold_boundary_audit.csv` and independently recomputed from `lightgbm_oos_predictions.csv`. `test_final_fold_absorbs_the_remainder_without_dropping_rows` asserts every non-final fold has identical test size, the final fold is `>=` that size, and no row is scored by more than one fold. Downstream aggregation correctly avoids the naive-average trap: `E2-S2`'s "overall" metrics are computed by pooling all 3,905 raw OOS rows and scoring once (`mae(overall_y_true, overall_y_pred)`), not by averaging six per-fold MAEs as if every fold carried equal weight — so the larger final fold is implicitly, correctly weighted by row count.

## 9. Edge case — full-sample preprocessing (cross-check against §5) — **Pass**

Repo-wide adversarial grep for `.mean(`, `.std(`, `.quantile(`, `StandardScaler(...).fit(`, `fit_transform`, `SelectKBest`, `PCA(` computed on an unsliced frame: the only genuine full-sample-vs-fold-scoped comparison found is `E2-S3/tests/test_walk_forward_validation.py::test_fold_scoped_scaler_differs_from_a_full_sample_scaler`, which *deliberately* fits a full-sample `StandardScaler` **inside a test**, specifically to prove by counter-example that it diverges from the correct fold-scoped one — it is a demonstration of the failure mode, not an instance of it in production code. The one genuine full-sample-shaped statistic in the real pipeline — the regime-threshold's `.expanding().median()` — was independently checked by `E4-S1` with a non-vacuity counterfactual: a literal full-sample-median version of the same threshold disagrees with the published (correct, `.shift(1)`-guarded) labels on 260 rows, proving the actual implementation is not silently full-sample. No EDA-derived global statistic was found reused inside a production preprocessing step.

## 10. Edge case — fold logic changed after seeing results (meta-leakage on the splitter itself) — **Pass, with one adjacent QA defect disclosed**

`git log -p --all -- E2-S1_Baseline_Zero_Predictor/splits.py` shows **exactly one commit** in the file's entire history (`b8eec2f`) — `HORIZON_TRADING_DAYS = 5`, `MIN_TRAIN_SIZE = 1260`, `N_FOLDS = 6` have never changed since creation, on any branch. There is no purge-length-shortened-because-results-were-disappointing scenario available even in principle here, because there is no second data point in git history to have changed *to*. This is structurally checked, not just asserted: `check_fold_params_unchanged_across_outputs()` compares `splits.py`'s **current** constants against the `split_params` **recorded inside** `baseline_zero_summary.json` and `lightgbm_summary.json` at the time those OOS numbers were produced — currently `MATCH` for both, per the committed `walk_forward_validation_summary.json`. First-principles justification is present: purge length is explicitly tied to the label horizon (§3/§4), not to any observed model score.

**One adjacent finding, disclosed for completeness rather than treated as a splitter defect**: the *gate script* that performs this check had a real bug of its own. `git diff b8eec2f b0cbde8 -- validate_walk_forward.py` shows the original commit hardcoded `"verdict": "PASS"` in the output JSON unconditionally — `compute_verdict()` did not exist yet, so a genuine parameter mismatch or an inadequate first fold would have been recorded in the JSON's detail fields but the top-line `verdict` would have read "PASS" regardless. This was found and fixed in `b0cbde8`, with a test (`test_compute_verdict_blocks_on_a_fold_params_mismatch`) whose docstring names the exact defect: *"Before this fix, `verdict` stayed hardcoded 'PASS' even here."* This is not evidence the splitter's parameters were ever actually adjusted post-hoc — the recorded values never disagreed — but it means that for the window between `b8eec2f` and `b0cbde8`, this particular safety net could not have been trusted to catch drift had it occurred. Distinguish clearly: the splitter (Object under audit) is unaffected; its own audit tooling briefly was not load-bearing.

**Same fold object reused everywhere**: confirmed by import, not convention. `run_baseline.py`, `train_lightgbm.py`, `validate_walk_forward.py`, `audit_oos_split_integrity.py`, and `E3-S2/pipeline/model.py` all contain `from splits import purged_walk_forward_splits` (the pipeline path adds `sys.path.insert` to reach the same file rather than vendoring a copy) — five consumers, one function body, confirmed by direct source read of every import line, not inferred from naming.

## Overall verdict: **Pass**

Every section passes on direct evidence — algebraic re-derivation of the purge arithmetic, cross-checked against two independently-generated, dated artefacts rather than the splitter's own self-report — and the §10 parameter-history review found a single-commit history with no post-hoc adjustment, structurally enforced by a cross-check against the two downstream summaries it's meant to protect. The one caveat on method (no live `pandas` execution available in this session; verification relied on algebraic derivation plus artefact cross-checking rather than a fresh run) is disclosed above rather than absorbed silently into the Pass. The one adjacent finding (§10's now-fixed hardcoded-PASS bug in the gate script) does not implicate the splitter itself and is already remediated in the current `main`.

### Recommendation carried forward, not a blocker for this audit

This splitter is sound and is *not* where the pipeline's problems live. The defects worth tracking are upstream/downstream of it: the previously-audited LightGBM stage duplication with a dropped seed (see the E2-S2 audit report) sits entirely on top of these folds without altering them — which is precisely why this splitter audit, taken alone, is a clean **Pass**: it correctly does its one job regardless of what the model-training stage built on top of it does with its output.
